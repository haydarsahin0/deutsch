## 1. What the numbers mean (realistic, day 1)

**Short #1 is passing its first tests.** YouTube shows a new Short to a small seed audience, measures how many
people *watch* instead of *swipe away* and how much of it they watch, then releases a bigger batch if the signals
are good. That is exactly what the charts show:
- 48-h chart: hourly views ≈ 105 → 215 → 230 → 300 (last bar is a partial hour). Each hour bigger than the last = YouTube keeps widening the audience.
- 60-min chart: two separate bursts (peaks ≈ 15–16 views/min) with a quiet gap in between = two test batches. A batch that ends and is followed by a *bigger* one is a good sign.
- ~950 views in a few hours for a 20-subscriber channel is a solid start. It is **not yet** "viral". Typical paths from here: it plateaus somewhere between 1 k and 5 k (most common for a first Short), or – if the swipe-away rate is very good – it gets pushed again in waves over the next 2–10 days (Shorts often come back days later). We can't tell which yet; the "viewed vs. swiped away" number decides it.
- Note: since 2025 YouTube counts a Shorts view on every start/replay. "Engaged views" (the old count) are lower – compare those when judging.

**The long video at 13 views is normal.** A long video on a brand-new channel has no subscribers to notify, no watch
history for the algorithm, and no search ranking yet. Long videos usually grow slowly through search + "suggested"
over weeks, not on day 1. Right now its only realistic feeder is Short #1 – and only if the **related-video link** is set (see action 1). With 940 Short views and 13 long views, the funnel Short → long is basically not working yet.

**Subscriber conversion: 4 subs / ~950 views ≈ 0.4 %.** Commonly reported ranges: Shorts ≈ 0.1–1 % (many channels
sit at 0.2–0.5 %), long videos ≈ 1–3 %+. So 0.4 % is a normal/decent Shorts value. Honest caveat: 4 is a tiny number
(one more or one less changes the rate a lot), and we don't know yet whether the subs came from the Short, the long
video or the channel page – Studio will show "subscribers gained" per video after ~24–48 h.

## 2. What to check in YouTube Studio next (and where)

Desktop Studio is easier (studio.youtube.com); the app has the same data under Analytics. Turkish UI labels are approximate.

**Short #1** – Content (İçerik) → Shorts → the Short → Analytics (Analiz):
- **Viewed vs. swiped away** (Engagement/Etkileşim tab, "İzlendi / Kaydırıldı"): the single most important Shorts number. Rough guide: < 60 % weak, 60–70 % ok, 70–80 % good, > 80 % very strong.
- **Average percentage viewed** + retention graph: > 80 % good, > 100 % means people loop/rewatch (great). Look for the second where the curve drops – that is the part to fix in the next Shorts.
- **Subscribers gained** and **engaged views** (Overview).
- Later (when available): related-video clicks / the long video's traffic from "Shorts feed".

**Long video** – Content → Videos → the video → Analytics:
- **Impressions & CTR** (Reach/Erişim tab, "Gösterimlerin tıklama oranı"): new channels typically 2–10 %; < 2 % → thumbnail/title problem.
- **Traffic source types** (Reach tab): look for **"Shorts feed"** (= clicks from the related-video link under the Short), Browse, Suggested, YouTube search, Channel pages.
- **Average view duration / % viewed** (Engagement tab): for an 11:34 tutorial 30–40 % is ok, 50 %+ is very good.
- **Retention graph – key moments**: first 30 s (should stay > 65–70 %), and drops at chapter changes / Kernfragen (quiz pauses!). Note the timestamps of big drops.

**Channel** (only from ~7 days on, too little data before): Audience (Kitle) → top countries, age, returning vs. new viewers.

## 4. Next long-video topics (high search demand for B1–C1)

Search demand here is an informed estimate (evergreen learner queries), not measured – verify quickly with YouTube
search autocomplete and Studio → Analytics → Research ("Recherche") before writing. All fit the existing 24 sets + 10 cast; no engine work.

1. **„Das sagt KEIN Deutscher! – Teil 2: Verben mit Präpositionen“** (warten *auf*, denken *an*, sich freuen *auf/über*, Angst *vor*, sich interessieren *für*, träumen *von* …). Why: "Verben mit Präpositionen" is a constant B1/B2 exam search, and a sequel reuses the format that is already working (title/thumbnail brand recognition). 5 Shorts: „Ich denke über dich“ (romantic disaster), „Ich freue mich auf / über“, „Ich habe Angst von…“, „Ich bin interessiert in…“, „Ich träume über…“ – each = wrong sentence + reaction + fix.
2. **„10 Redewendungen, die Deutsche JEDEN Tag benutzen“** (Ich verstehe nur Bahnhof, Das ist nicht mein Bier, Schwein haben, Tomaten auf den Augen, Daumen drücken, Da steppt der Bär …). Why: "deutsche Redewendungen" is one of the biggest learner queries and very visual (literal image vs. meaning = perfect for our ink illustrations and the cast). 5 Shorts: the 5 funniest literal-vs-real scenes, hook „Was bedeutet DAS?“.
3. **„doch, mal, halt, eben, ja – die kleinen Wörter, die dich deutsch klingen lassen“** (Modalpartikeln). Why: core B2–C1 topic, little good German-only competition, fits our "sound natural" brand; follows naturally from the „Doch“ Short. 5 Shorts: one per particle („Komm mal her“ vs. „Komm her“, „Ist halt so“, „Das ist ja toll!“ …), hook „Warum sagen Deutsche das?“.
